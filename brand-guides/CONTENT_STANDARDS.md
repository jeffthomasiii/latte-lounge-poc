# Content Standards

**Purpose:** Define how Latte & Lounge sounds, what it talks about, how content is structured, and how Scripture, founder voice, and calls to action are handled.

---

## 1. Core Voice

Latte & Lounge should sound like **Samatra**.

Primary voice reference:

> A trusted auntie in the kitchen who loves God, tells the truth, listens without judgment, and shares what she has learned because she genuinely wants another woman to grow.

The voice is:

- Warm
- Direct
- Honest
- Conversational
- Scripture-rooted
- Emotionally aware
- Practical
- Nonjudgmental
- Familiar
- Spiritually confident
- Unpretentious

The voice is not:

- Condescending
- Performative
- Spiritually superior
- Overly polished
- Corporate
- Artificially inspirational
- Formulaic
- Detached
- Preachy
- Judgmental

### The Reader Relationship

Write to one capable woman who may be carrying more than other people realize. Do not talk down to her, idealize her, or assume she has failed because she feels tired, uncertain, or inconsistent.

Invite her into honesty with God. Do not position Latte & Lounge as the solution God Himself provides.

---

## 2. Audience Language

### Internal Audience Definition

Professional Christian women, generally ages 30–55, who integrate faith with work, leadership, relationships, caregiving, and everyday responsibility.

### Preferred Public Language

> Professional women who love God and carry a lot.

Use the public wording when a human, emotionally resonant description is more helpful than a demographic label.

### Audience Assumptions to Avoid

Do not assume every reader:

- Is married
- Has children
- Holds a formal leadership title
- Works in a corporate office
- Begins her day at the same time
- Has an hour for devotion
- Is emotionally well
- Knows church terminology
- Has a polished prayer life
- Shares Samatra’s exact experiences

---

## 3. Messaging Hierarchy

Most Latte & Lounge content should move through this sequence:

1. **Recognition** — Name what she may be carrying.
2. **Invitation** — Encourage her to bring it honestly to God.
3. **Scripture** — Ground the conversation in the biblical text.
4. **Wisdom** — Connect Scripture to work, leadership, relationships, or ordinary life.
5. **Response** — Offer one practical action, reflection, or prayer.
6. **Release** — Leave her encouraged and grounded, not burdened with another checklist.

A piece does not need to label every step, but the reader should experience the progression.

Avoid wording that feels:

## 6. Blog and Reflection Standards

### Preferred Structure

```text
Story or Recognition
→ Scripture
→ Honest Reflection
→ Practical Wisdom
→ Action, Question, or Prayer
```

### Typical Length

- Short reflection: approximately 500–800 words
- Standard article: approximately 800–1,200 words
- Longer interview or teaching: as needed, with strong subheadings and editing

Length should follow the idea. Do not expand a simple thought to meet an arbitrary count.

### Opening

Begin with a recognizable experience, honest observation, specific story, or meaningful question. Avoid generic openings such as “In today’s fast-paced world.”

### Scripture

Introduce Scripture naturally and explain why it matters in the specific situation. Do not use a verse as decoration or as a shortcut around thoughtful application.

### Application

Offer practical wisdom without turning every article into a list of productivity steps. One clear response may be more useful than five shallow tips.

### Closing

End with one of the following:

- A short prayer
- A reflection question
- A journal prompt
- One practical next step
- A gentle invitation to continue with a related resource

Do not force every piece into the exact same ending.

## 7. Prayer Content Standards

Prayer content should be:

- Honest
- Direct
- Reverent
- Scripture-aware
- Emotionally specific
- Free from performance

### Recommended Flow

1. Address God naturally.
2. Name what is true.
3. Acknowledge dependence on Him.
4. Ask for wisdom, peace, courage, correction, or help.
5. Surrender the outcome.
6. Close with trust rather than certainty that a preferred result will occur.

Avoid guaranteeing outcomes God has not promised. Do not use prayer as a disguised lecture to the reader.

## 8. Podcast Standards

### Target Experience

The podcast should feel like Samatra is speaking to one woman during a real morning—not delivering a stage performance.

### Typical Length

Approximately 5–15 minutes for short reflections and prayers. Interviews and substantive conversations may be longer.

### Suggested Structure

1. Warm welcome
2. Today’s honest thought or situation
3. Scripture anchor
4. Conversation and practical wisdom
5. Prayer or reflection
6. Gentle next step

### Podcast Identity

- **Name:** Latte & Lounge
- **Subtitle:** Where Women Meet God Before the World Gets Loud

Avoid overproduced intros, excessive promotional breaks, and language that sounds like broadcast radio rather than a personal conversation.

## 9. Founder Voice and Biography

Samatra’s story is central to the brand, but biography must remain accurate and appropriately limited.

Current supported founder themes include:

- Latte & Lounge developed during a demanding professional leadership transition.
- Greater responsibility made the need for God’s direction more visible.
- Honest morning conversations with God changed the trajectory of the day.
- She wants to share practical and spiritual wisdom that may help another woman be better prepared.

Do not invent:

- Employers or job titles
- Credentials
- Family details
- Ministry roles
- Dates
- Locations
- Personal hardships
- Testimonies or theological claims Samatra has not provided

When new founder details are approved, update the relevant page and this guide if the detail becomes part of the recurring brand story.

---

## 10. Scripture Standards

- Confirm the reference and wording before publication.
- Identify the translation when quoting Scripture in long-form content or when permissions require it.
- Preserve the verse’s meaning and context.
- Do not build a major conclusion on a fragment detached from its passage.
- Distinguish clearly between what the text says, Samatra’s reflection, and practical application.
- Avoid implying a universal promise where Scripture does not provide one.
- Use Scripture respectfully, not as decorative filler in every graphic.

The project should establish a preferred default Bible translation before large-scale publication. Until then, record the translation used in each draft.

---

## 11. Headlines and Titles

Headlines should be:

- Clear
- Human
- Emotionally recognizable
- Specific enough to create interest
- Consistent with the actual content

Prefer:

- Before the Difficult Conversation
- When Leadership Feels Lonely
- Bring God What You Are Carrying
- Excellence Without Exhaustion
- What Do You Need to Set Down This Morning?

Avoid:

- Clickbait
- Vague inspiration
- Fear-based urgency
- Overpromising outcomes
- Excessive punctuation
- Titles that sound like corporate seminars

Use sentence case or editorial title case consistently within a content collection.

---

## 12. Calls to Action

Calls to action should feel like the natural continuation of the conversation.

Preferred types:

- Read the reflection
- Listen to the episode
- Pray with me
- Explore the prayer resources
- Meet Samatra
- Start your morning
- Join the Lounge
- Bring this question to your journal

Avoid stacked conversion requests. A page should usually have one primary next step and, when needed, one quieter alternative.

Do not promote email signup, podcast platforms, events, products, mentorship, coaching, or community offerings as functional until they are actually available.

---

## 13. Web Copy Standards

### Scannability

- Use clear headings.
- Keep introductory paragraphs focused.
- Use lists when they improve comprehension, not to make every idea look tactical.
- Use descriptive links rather than repeated “click here” wording.

### Metadata

Each page or content item should include, as applicable:

- Accurate title
- Clear description or excerpt
- Featured image
- Meaningful image alt text
- Publication date
- Category or pillar
- Scripture reference and translation when quoted

### Search and Sharing

Descriptions should explain the real value of the page in natural language. Do not pack metadata with keywords or rewrite Samatra’s voice for search engines.

---

## 14. Editorial Review Checklist

Before publishing, confirm:

### Voice

- It sounds like Samatra rather than a generic devotional writer.
- It is warm, direct, honest, and nonjudgmental.
- It avoids corporate, performative, and overly churchy language.

### Message

- The reader’s real situation is recognized.
- God remains the source of wisdom and grounding.
- The content does not shame inconsistency or emotional struggle.
- The application is practical without becoming productivity-first.

### Scripture

- References and quotations are accurate.
- The translation is recorded where needed.
- The application respects context.
- Personal interpretation is not presented as the only possible reading without support.

### Accuracy

- Founder details are supported.
- Current and future offerings are represented honestly.
- Links, dates, names, and resource details are correct.

### Presentation

- The title matches the content.
- The opening is specific and engaging.
- Headings support readability.
- The ending provides a fitting response or next step.
- The featured image and alt text support the message.

### Final Test

Ask:

> Will this help a woman meet honestly with God and carry her day differently, or will it simply give her more to perform?

Publish only when the content supports the first outcome.
