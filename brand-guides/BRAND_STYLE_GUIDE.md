# Brand Style Guide

**Purpose:** Define how Latte & Lounge should look across the website, podcast, social content, email, presentations, and future products.

---

## 1. Visual Direction

Latte & Lounge combines **early-morning stillness, warm editorial design, feminine sophistication, and professional clarity**.

The visual system should feel:

- Warm
- Elevated
- Peaceful
- Feminine
- Modern
- Grounded
- Professional
- Sophisticated
- Intentional
- Quietly luxurious

It should not feel:

- Rustic farmhouse
- Overly floral
- Ornate or decorative
- Bohemian
- Juvenile
- Loud or busy
- Corporate sterile
- Trendy for trend’s sake
- Hyper-minimal to the point of coldness

The benchmark is an elevated coffee ritual in a calm, modern space—not a busy coffee shop, church bulletin, or lifestyle-influencer feed.

## 2. Official Color Palette

| Color | HEX | Primary Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cream | `#F6F2ED` | Primary backgrounds and visual breathing room |
| Latte Beige | `#E7D6C2` | Warm section backgrounds, cards, and soft fills |
| Soft Mocha | `#8B6E5A` | Core brand brown, icons, structure, and supporting type |
| Warm Taupe | `#8B7D6B` | Secondary text and muted contrast |
| Muted Gold | `#C8A668` | Fine lines, separators, and premium details |
| Terracotta | `#D36A5C` | Primary calls to action and signature emphasis |
| Sage | `#A4B59B` | Calm supporting accent used sparingly |

### Recommended Ratio

- **70%** Cream + Latte Beige
- **20%** Soft Mocha + Warm Taupe
- **8%** Terracotta
- **2%** Muted Gold
- Sage is optional and should remain secondary.

### Color Behavior

- Use Cream as the dominant canvas.
- Use Latte Beige to create warmth without turning every section into a card.
- Use Terracotta for actions and selective emphasis, not large portions of every page.
- Use Muted Gold as a detail, never as a dominant fill.
- Use Sage as a breath of calm, not as a competing primary color.
- Avoid pure black when Soft Mocha or the site’s deeper warm ink provides adequate contrast.

### Accessibility

Brand colors do not automatically guarantee readable contrast. Verify text and interactive states against WCAG contrast requirements. Terracotta, Muted Gold, Sage, and Latte Beige should not be used for small text unless the final foreground/background combination passes contrast testing.

## 3. Typography

### Official Brand Direction

| Role | Typeface | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display serif | Canela | Logo, major headlines, editorial titles, premium print applications |
| Supporting sans serif | Avenir Next | Body text, navigation, buttons, captions, email, and UI |
| Accent script | Mademoiselle | Ampersand, brief callouts, and rare personal accents |

### Current Website Implementation

The website currently uses free Google Fonts that preserve the intended character:

| Role | Current Web Typeface | Relationship to Brand Direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display serif | Cormorant Garamond | Practical editorial alternative to Canela |
| Body and UI | Montserrat | Practical modern alternative to Avenir Next |
| Accent script | Sacramento | Practical script alternative used sparingly |

The distinction must remain clear: Canela, Avenir Next, and Mademoiselle define the aspirational identity; Cormorant Garamond, Montserrat, and Sacramento are the present website implementation.

### Typography Rules

- Use the display serif for headlines, large quotes, and editorial emphasis.
- Use the sans serif for all body copy, navigation, metadata, buttons, forms, and accessibility-critical text.
- Use script only for a short accent, signature, or ampersand.
- Never set paragraphs, navigation, instructions, or small text in script.
- Use generous line height and comfortable line length.
- Avoid long passages in all caps. Reserve uppercase tracking for labels, navigation, metadata, and short calls to action.

## 4. Logo System

### Primary Wordmark

The primary website wordmark is stored at:

`docs/assets/images/logo-latte-lounge-wordmark.png`

Use it for:

- Website header and footer
- Brand-guide covers
- Presentation covers
- Social headers
- Formal brand applications

### Coffee Cup Icon

The official simplified icon is the coffee cup taken directly from the Latte & Lounge logo. It should preserve the approved cup shape rather than being reinterpreted as a different coffee symbol.

Use it for:

- Favicon
- Social avatar where the full wordmark is too small
- Compact app or browser contexts
- Small supporting marks

The favicon currently uses:

`docs/assets/images/favicon.svg`

**Do not place an unnecessary circle or badge around the cup.** A containing shape may only be used when a platform technically requires one and should not become part of the icon itself.

### Logo Clear Space

Leave clear space around the logo at least equal to the visible height of the coffee cup handle. The logo should never feel crowded by navigation, borders, photography, or other text.

### Logo Misuse

Do not:

- Stretch, compress, rotate, or skew the logo
- Rebuild the wordmark with substitute fonts
- Change individual logo colors casually
- Add drop shadows, outlines, bevels, or effects
- Place it over visually busy photography without a protective field
- Enclose the favicon cup in a decorative circle by default
- Use a generic coffee icon in place of the approved cup

## 5. Photography

Photography should communicate a real moment of preparation, reflection, or honest connection.

### Preferred Qualities

- Early morning or soft natural light
- Warm neutral color temperature
- Modern interiors
- Textured ceramics, linen, wood, stone, paper, and subtle brass
- Coffee, journals, Scripture, and work objects used naturally
- Women shown as thoughtful, capable, and real
- Quiet moments rather than overt posing
- Editorial composition with room for text when needed
- Professional life represented without generic boardroom stock imagery

### Avoid

- Fake smiling office teams
- Staged prayer poses
- Excessive crosses, florals, or church clichés
- Over-filtered influencer photography
- Cluttered desktops and busy backgrounds
- Rustic farmhouse props
- Coffee and Bible arrangements that feel artificial or commercial
- Images that imply a perfect morning is required to meet with God

### Image Treatment

- Favor natural contrast and warm highlights.
- Preserve skin tone accuracy.
- Avoid heavy presets and crushed shadows.
- Use consistent aspect ratios within a component family.
- Provide meaningful alt text describing the image’s purpose and content.

---

## 6. Graphic Language

### Shapes

- Favor clean rectangles with restrained rounded corners.
- Use asymmetry selectively for editorial energy.
- Avoid excessive pills, bubbles, decorative blobs, and layered ornaments.
- Full pill shapes are appropriate for small badges only, not every component.

### Lines and Dividers

- Fine Muted Gold or low-opacity Mocha lines may organize content.
- Dividers should be subtle and functional.
- The coffee-bean divider may be used as a recognizable brand detail but should not appear repeatedly on every page.

### Icons

- Use simple, recognizable marks with consistent visual weight.
- Prefer line or restrained solid icons.
- Avoid mixing emoji, unrelated icon styles, and ornate illustrations in production components.
- Custom brand icons should use Soft Mocha, Terracotta, Sage, or Muted Gold with restraint.

### Texture

Subtle paper, linen, ceramic, stone, or warm-grain references may add tactility. Texture should be quiet enough that typography remains the focus.

---

## 7. Layout and Composition

- Use generous whitespace.
- Let photography and typography carry the visual hierarchy.
- Keep content widths comfortable for reading.
- Create rhythm through alternating open sections, editorial split layouts, and restrained card groups.
- Avoid placing every section inside a box.
- Use strong alignment and repeatable spacing rather than decorative filler.
- Allow one clear focal point per section.

Quiet luxury is created through proportion, restraint, typography, and material feeling—not by adding more decoration.

---

## 8. Brand Applications

### Website

Warm editorial layouts, restrained cards, clear calls to action, readable long-form content, and a polished but welcoming atmosphere.

### Podcast

Square artwork should prioritize the Latte & Lounge name and the subtitle **Where women meet God before the world gets loud.** Avoid dense episode-cover templates.

### Social Content

Use spacious templates, one strong message, editorial typography, and a limited palette. Social graphics should not rely on the logo as the only sign of brand consistency.

### Email

Favor clear type, warm neutrals, short headings, and a personal note-like rhythm. Email should feel conversational rather than campaign-heavy.

### Presentations and Documents

Use the primary wordmark on covers, Cream backgrounds, Soft Mocha or warm ink for text, Terracotta for selective emphasis, Muted Gold rules, and spacious layouts.

### Future Products

Journals, prayer cards, and devotionals should feel modern, giftable, calming, and premium. Appropriate material cues include linen, matte paper, soft-touch finishes, blind embossing, restrained foil, and natural texture.

---

## 9. Visual Approval Checklist

Before approving an asset, verify:

- The official logo or icon has been used correctly.
- Cream and warm neutrals dominate the composition.
- Terracotta, Gold, and Sage are restrained.
- Typography follows the role hierarchy.
- Script is used only as an accent.
- Photography feels natural, warm, modern, and honest.
- The layout has enough breathing room.
- The design feels feminine and professional without becoming decorative or corporate.
- Text contrast and interactive states are accessible.
- The result supports the message rather than overpowering it.
